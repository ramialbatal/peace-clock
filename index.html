    How we calculate this

    Governments report military spending per year, not per second. When this page opens it downloads SIPRI's latest yearly figures (currently up to ), projects them to using each country's latest year-on-year change (capped at 25% a year), and spreads the result evenly over the year. All amounts are in US dollars at 2024 prices, so years can be compared fairly.

    growth   = S[latest] / S[latest - 1] - 1
annual   = S[latest] × (1 + growth)^(this year - latest)
so far   = annual × fraction of the year elapsed (UTC)

    Lives lost are the Uppsala Conflict Data Program's best estimate of soldiers and civilians killed in armed conflicts: wars between countries, wars within countries, fighting between armed groups, and deliberate attacks on civilians. The counter for this year assumes the pace of the latest complete year (). It is not a live count of today's wars.

    What these numbers count

    Military spending

    Everything governments spend on their armed forces: soldiers' pay and pensions, weapons, bases, research, and military aid to other countries. The Stockholm International Peace Research Institute (SIPRI) collects it for almost every country.

    Lives lost to war

    People killed in the fighting, soldiers and civilians, counted by the Uppsala Conflict Data Program from news, reports and local sources. Many more die later from hunger, illness and lost homes, so the real cost of war is much higher.
